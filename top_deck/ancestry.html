I

Ancestry

Your ancestry represents the people, culture, and natural talents you were born with. Ancestry provides a small number of specialized abilities without determining your character's class or core attributes.

Ancestry Does Not Modify Attributes

Ancestry never changes Might, Spirit, Dexterity, or Cunning. Your character's attributes are determined separately from ancestry.

During character creation, choose one ancestry. Your ancestry provides two ancestry feats. Choose two of the available feats listed for your ancestry.

```
```
II

Choosing Ancestry Feats

  1. Choose your character's ancestry.
  2. Review the two ancestry feats available to that ancestry.
  3. Choose both ancestry feats.
  4. Record the feats on your character sheet.

Ancestry feats are permanent character features unless a later rule specifically allows a character to change them.

```
```
III

Human

Humans are adaptable, ambitious, and remarkably capable of learning whatever their circumstances demand. Their strength comes not from a single defining trait, but from versatility.

Skilled

Gain Proficiency in one skill of your choice.

Shielder

Gain Proficiency with shields.

```
```
IV

Elf

Elves are graceful and attuned to the subtle forces of the world. Their traditions often emphasize precision, magic, and movement.

Spell Weaver

Learn one Tier 1 spell of your choice.

The spell must be one your character is otherwise capable of learning or casting.

Nimble Weapons

Gain Proficiency with Club weapons.

```
```
V

Dwarf

Dwarves are renowned for endurance, martial discipline, and the ability to withstand hardships that would break others.

Strong Fighter

Gain Proficiency with Spade weapons.

Dwarven Health

Gain 1 Heart.

A Heart represents an additional point of character health.

```
```
VI

Halfling

Halflings are quick, fortunate, and often underestimated. Their greatest strength is their ability to turn a bad situation into an opportunity.

Lucky

When you draw an Ace, you may redraw it.

The redrawn card replaces the Ace for the purpose of resolving the action or check.

The Ace is still handled according to the normal Ace rules when it is not redrawn.

Cheerful

When you use Rallying Cry, increase the number of cards drawn by the ability.

This additional card is drawn by the ally receiving the Rallying Cry benefit.

VIII

Design Principles

Ancestry is intentionally lightweight. It should establish character identity without determining the character's role.

  • Ancestry does not modify core attributes.
  • Every ancestry provides exactly two feats.
  • Both ancestry feats are selected during character creation.
  • Ancestry abilities should remain useful across multiple classes.
  • Ancestry should complement, rather than replace, class abilities.
  • The strongest ancestry abilities should create new tactical options rather than simply adding large numerical bonuses.
```
```
IX

Ancestry Playtest Questions

  • Are all four ancestries attractive choices for multiple classes?
  • Does Dwarven Health provide an appropriate benefit compared with the other ancestry feats?
  • Is Spell Weaver too restrictive if it requires a character to already have access to spells?
  • Should Spell Weaver allow an Elf to learn any Tier 1 spell, regardless of class?
  • Does Lucky interact cleanly with the Ace and Gobble mechanics?
  • Is Cheerful sufficiently distinct from other card-draw abilities?
  • Are weapon proficiencies valuable enough to make Strong Fighter and Nimble Weapons meaningful choices?
  • Does Skilled compete appropriately with the more specialized ancestry feats?
