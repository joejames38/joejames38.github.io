Realm & Rune
A Card-Driven Fantasy Roleplaying Game
I

Core Rules

Realm & Rune is a lightweight heroic fantasy tabletop roleplaying game built around standard playing cards instead of dice.

Each player has their own deck of cards. Cards represent both uncertainty and resources. Players must decide whether to spend powerful cards now or preserve them for a future challenge.

Core Design Principle

The game is built around simple arithmetic, meaningful card choices, asymmetric characters, and fast resolution.

II

What You Need

  • One standard 52-card deck for each player.
  • Optional Jokers for the optional critical-fumble rule.
  • Character sheets.
  • Pencils or other writing tools.
  • A way to track HP and other temporary effects.
Playtest Rule: Starting deck composition remains a playtest variable. New or low-level characters may use a reduced deck containing only number cards.
III

The Card System

Every player maintains a personal deck throughout the game. Cards move between several zones as they are used.

Card Value Rule
2–10 Printed value Used normally for attacks, checks, and abilities.
Jack 10 May trigger a face-card effect.
Queen 10 May trigger a face-card effect.
King 10 May trigger a face-card effect.
Ace 1 Enable the Gobble mechanic.
Joker Optional Jokers are not part of the core rules. They may be added as critical fumbles.
IV

Card Zones

Deck

The active draw pile. Cards are drawn from the Deck whenever a rule calls for a draw.

Hand

Cards currently available for the player to spend. Hand size increases as characters become more powerful.

Shuffle Pile

Cards used for ordinary actions, attacks, reactions, and skill checks are placed here.

Burn Pile

Cards used to cast spells are permanently removed from normal circulation until recovered by resting.

Deck Depletion

When the Deck is empty, shuffle the Shuffle Pile and make it the new Deck. Cards in the Burn Pile remain unavailable.

V

Hand Size

A character's Hand Size determines how many cards they may retain between turns.

Level Maximum Hand Size
1–2 2
3–4 3
5 4

Certain ancestry feats, class features, subclass feats, and other abilities may temporarily or permanently modify the number of cards a character can draw or retain.

VI

Scenes

The game uses a flexible scene structure rather than requiring every situation to be combat.

Combat

Characters fight enemies while managing movement, attacks, abilities, and card resources.

Social Scenes

Characters use Spirit-based skills and other abilities to influence NPCs and navigate relationships.

Exploration

Characters investigate locations, overcome obstacles, discover secrets, and manage resources.

Skill Challenges

Characters use their skills to overcome difficult situations where failure has meaningful consequences.

VII

Starting a Scene

At the beginning of a structured scene, each player draws cards until their Hand reaches their maximum Hand Size.

These cards become the character's available resources for the scene.

Important: A character does not automatically refill their Hand at the beginning of every turn.
VIII

Turn Structure

  1. Start of Turn: Draw one card from your Deck.
  2. Move: You may move. Movement does not require a card.
  3. Actions: You may play cards to perform actions. Normally, each card played provides one action.
  4. Reactions: Reactions may be used when their triggering condition occurs. A card must normally be played to trigger a reaction.
  5. End of Turn: If you have more cards than your Hand Size, discard cards until you reach your maximum.
Explosive Turns

Players are allowed to spend multiple cards during their turn. Drawing additional cards through abilities can create powerful turns, but spending those cards also reduces the player's future options.

IX

Action Economy

Resource Cost
Move No card required.
Action Normally one card.
Reaction Normally one card.
Free Activity Talking to other characters and when specifically allowed by a rule.

Unless an ability says otherwise, every action or reaction requires a card to be played.

X

Core Attributes

Every character has four core attributes ranging from -1 to +3.

Attribute Suit General Uses
Might ♠ Spades Strength, endurance, melee combat, physical force.
Spirit ♥ Hearts Courage, leadership, healing, social actions.
Dexterity ♣ Clubs Agility, precision, movement, risk.
Cunning ♦ Diamonds Knowledge, quick thinking, investigation, magical prowess.
Suit Affinity: Suits determine the abilities, skills, weapons, and magical domains associated with an attribute. Suits do not automatically provide an additional numerical modifier.
XI

Checks

When a character attempts something uncertain where failure matters, the GM calls for a check.

A check normally consists of a card value plus the appropriate attribute modifier.

Check Total
Card Value + Attribute Modifier

The GM determines the Difficulty of the check based on the circumstances.

Advantage and Disadvantage

Some circumstance may add favorable or unfortunate circumstances for characters.

Advantage: Play a card from your hand, and if you have Advantage, draw and play a card from the top of your deck, and take the MOST favorable result. This can causing a face card effect to trigger or suit resonance to be applied.

Disadvantage: Play a card from your hand, and if you have Disadvantage, draw and play a card from the top of your deck, and take the LEAST favorable result. This can cause losing a face card effect or losing suit resonance being applied.

XIII

Skill Training

Training Procedure
Untrained Disadvantage on the skill check.
Proficient Play one card from your Hand. Add the played skill's appropriate attribute modifier.
Expertise Play up to two cards from your Hand. If two cards are played, the second card must match the skill's suit affinity. Add both card values and the associated skill's attribute modifier.
Skill Cards: All cards used during skill checks are placed in the Shuffle Pile.
XIV

Face Cards

Jacks, Queens, and Kings always have a value of 10.

In addition to their value, a face card may activate a special effect when an ability specifically references a face card.

A face card is therefore both a strong numerical card and a potential source of special effects.
XV

Combat Summary

Combat follows the same card mechanics as other scenes. Detailed combat rules are maintained on the dedicated Combat page.

Attack Total
Card Value + Relevant Attribute Modifier

If the Attack Total equals or exceeds the target's Armor Class, the attack hits.

Face cards have a value of 10 and may activate special effects.

XVI

Armor Class

Defense AC
Unarmored 5 + Dexterity
Light Armor 6 + Dexterity
Medium Armor 7
Heavy Armor 9
Shield +1 AC

Examples

  • Unarmored character with Dexterity +0: AC 5
  • Unarmored character with Dexterity +3: AC 8
  • Light Armor with Dexterity +3: AC 9
  • Medium Armor with Shield: AC 8
  • Heavy Armor with Shield: AC 10

Most ordinary enemies should generally fall within an AC range of approximately 5–8. AC 9–10 represents strong mundane defense. Higher AC should generally be reserved for exceptional or supernatural defenses.

XVII

Defend & Dodge

Defend

Action: Discard a card and add its value to your AC until the beginning of your next turn.

The card is placed in the Shuffle Pile.

Dodge

Reaction: When hit, discard one Reserve card to negate 1 HP of damage.

The Reserve terminology is currently under development and will be standardized in the next rules pass.

XVIII

Rest

Short Rest

Shuffle all cards in the Shuffle Pile and Burn Pile back into the Deck.

Long Rest

Recover HP and shuffle all cards from the Shuffle Pile and Burn Pile back into the Deck.

XIX

Magic

Magic is divided into four domains. Detailed spellcasting rules and spell lists are maintained on the dedicated Magic page.

Domain Suit Theme
Arcane ♦ Knowledge, elements, force, magical manipulation.
Divine ♥ Healing, protection, blessings, judgment.
Primal ♣ Nature, beasts, storms, transformation.
Occult ♠ Secrets, spirits, curses, shadows, forbidden power.
Spell Resource: Cards used to cast spells are placed in the Burn Pile.
XX

Character Creation

  1. Choose an Ancestry.
  2. Choose two available Ancestry Feats.
  3. Choose a Background.
  4. Apply the Background's skill proficiencies and feature.
  5. Choose a Class.
  6. Apply the Level 1 class features.
  7. Determine your core attributes.
  8. Record starting equipment, weapons, armor, skills, spells, and other abilities.

Ancestry does not modify core attributes.

XXI

Advancement

The maximum character level is Level 5.

Level Universal Advancement Hand Size
1 Gain class identity features. 2
2 Gain a subclass feature. 2
3 Gain a class identity feature. 3
4 Gain a subclass feature. 3
5 Increase one core attribute by +1. 4
Asymmetric Growth

Characters do not need to follow identical development paths. Class identity provides the foundation, while subclass feats and other feats allow characters with the same class to develop substantially different play styles.

XXII

Subclass Growth

Subclasses are a major source of asymmetric character growth.

Rather than requiring every member of a subclass to receive exactly the same feature at the same level, each subclass is designed around a pool of independent subclass feats.

Subclass Design Principle

Each subclass should eventually contain approximately 10 independent features. Characters choose from those features as they advance, allowing two characters with the same class and subclass to play differently.

The exact acquisition rules for subclass feats will be defined on the dedicated Feats page.

XXIII

The Level Cap

Level 5 represents the end of traditional character leveling. The intent is for characters to become increasingly differentiated through feats rather than continually increasing numerical power.

Once a character reaches Level 5, future advancement comes from acquiring and spending Feat Points.

Feat Points may eventually be used to purchase:

  • Subclass feats
  • Ancestry feats
  • General feats
  • Additional skill proficiencies
  • Expertise
  • Weapon or armor proficiencies
  • New abilities
  • Other specialized character options
XXIV

Design Philosophy

Low Math

Most resolutions should require little more than adding a card value and an attribute modifier.

Meaningful Choices

Spending a strong card now should matter because that card will not be immediately available later.

Asymmetric Characters

Characters of the same class should be able to feel substantially different through feats and subclasses.

Heroic Fantasy

Characters should feel capable and powerful while still facing meaningful danger in combat and dungeon exploration.

XXV

Current Development Status

Realm & Rune is currently a playtest ruleset. The following systems are established enough for early testing but remain subject to revision:

  • Card values and card zones.
  • Hand size and card economy.
  • Core attributes.
  • Skill proficiency and expertise.
  • Basic action economy.
  • Armor Class.
  • Spell resource management.
  • Level 1–5 advancement structure.

Exact balance, starting deck composition, HP scaling, enemy design, spell balance, feat costs, and encounter difficulty require playtesting.
