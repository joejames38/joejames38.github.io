Realm & Rune
A Card-Driven Fantasy Roleplaying Game
I

Core Rules

Realm & Rune is a lightweight heroic fantasy tabletop roleplaying game built around standard playing cards instead of dice.

Each player has their own deck of cards. Cards represent both uncertainty and resources. Players must decide whether to spend powerful cards now or preserve them for a future challenge.

Your cards are both your dice and your action economy. Spending a card gives you an opportunity to act now, but reduces the cards available to you later.

The game is designed for heroic fantasy adventures, dungeon exploration, dangerous encounters, social scenes, and magical adventures.

II

What You Need

  • One standard 52-card deck for each player.
  • Optional Jokers for the optional critical-fumble rule.
  • Character sheets.
  • Pencils or other writing tools.
  • A way to track HP and other temporary effects.
Playtest Rule: Starting deck composition remains a playtest variable. New or low-level characters may use a reduced deck containing only number cards.
III

The Card System

Every player maintains a personal deck throughout the game. Cards move between several zones as they are used.

Card Values

Card Value Rule
2–10 Printed value Used normally for attacks, checks, and abilities.
Jack 10 May trigger a face-card effect.
Queen 10 May trigger a face-card effect.
King 10 May trigger a face-card effect.
Ace 1 Enable the Gobble mechanic.
Joker Optional Jokers are not part of the core rules. They may be added as critical fumbles.
IV

Card Zones

Deck

The active draw pile. Cards are drawn from the Deck whenever a rule calls for a draw.

Hand

Cards currently available for the player to spend. Hand size increases as characters become more powerful.

Shuffle Pile

Cards used for ordinary actions, attacks, reactions, and skill checks are placed here.

Burn Pile

Cards used to cast spells are permanently removed from normal circulation until recovered by resting.

Shuffle Pile and Burn Pile

Shuffle Pile

Cards normally go to the Shuffle Pile after they are played.

Deck Depletion

When the Deck is empty, shuffle the Shuffle Pile and make it the new Deck. Cards in the Burn Pile remain unavailable.

Burn Pile

Cards placed in the Burn Pile are removed from normal deck circulation.

Spells and other abilities may require cards to be Burned. Burning cards is a meaningful resource cost because those cards are no longer immediately available when the deck is reshuffled.

Play vs. Burn

Play normally sends a card to the Shuffle Pile.

Burn sends a card to the Burn Pile.

V

Core Attributes

Every character has four core attributes ranging from -1 to +3.

Attribute Suit General Uses
Might ♠ Spades Strength, endurance, melee combat, physical force.
Spirit ♥ Hearts Courage, leadership, healing, social actions.
Dexterity ♣ Clubs Agility, precision, movement, risk.
Cunning ♦ Diamonds Knowledge, quick thinking, investigation, magical prowess.
Suit Affinity: Suits determine the abilities, skills, weapons, and magical domains associated with an attribute. Suits do not automatically provide an additional numerical modifier.
VI

Hand Size

A character's Hand Size determines how many cards they may retain between turns.

Level Maximum Hand Size
1–2 2
3–4 3
5 4

Certain ancestry feats, class features, subclass feats, and other abilities may temporarily or permanently modify the number of cards a character can draw or retain.

VII

Scenes

The game uses a flexible scene structure rather than requiring every situation to be combat.

Combat

Characters fight enemies while managing movement, attacks, abilities, and card resources.

Social Scenes

Characters use Spirit-based skills and other abilities to influence NPCs and navigate relationships.

Exploration

Characters investigate locations, overcome obstacles, discover secrets, and manage resources.

Skill Challenges

Characters use their skills to overcome difficult situations where failure has meaningful consequences.

Beginning a Scene

At the beginning of a scene, draw cards until your hand reaches your maximum hand size.

Beginning Your Turn

At the beginning of your turn, draw one card.

Playing Cards

You may play as many cards as you are able to use during your turn, subject to your available actions and abilities.

Explosive Turns

Players are allowed to spend multiple cards during their turn. Drawing additional cards through abilities can create powerful turns, but spending those cards also reduces the player's future options.

Cards played for ordinary actions, attacks, skill checks, and other abilities are placed into the Shuffle Pile unless an ability specifically says that the card is Burned.

At the end of your turn, if you have more cards than your maximum hand size, play cards until you are back at your maximum.

There is no requirement to spend your entire hand.

Players must decide whether to spend cards for additional actions now or preserve valuable cards for later turns.

IX

Action Economy

Resource Cost
Move No card required. Instead of moving, you can also draw weapons or store and interact with items.
Action Normally one card from your hand.
Reaction Normally one card. Only one Reaction per turn.
Free Activity Talking to other characters.
Dropping an item in your hand.

Unless an ability says otherwise, every action or reaction requires a card to be played.

X

Movement

Each character receives one Move each turn.

Movement does not require a card and does not consume an Action.

Movement Distance

Exact movement distances may use grids, zones, or theater-of-the-mind in combat.

Close: Right next to you. Touching distance, toe-to-toe in melee combat. Moving to something Close takes zero movement.

Near: General room distance. Or about 30ft. A standard single movement allows you to travel from Close to Near.

Far: Across a large room, down the hall, or a long distance away. Reaching a Far target usually takes a full movement plus a bit more or a special effort, putting targets at the edge of ranged weapon capabilities.

Out of Range: Too far away to interact with or attack until you spend a turn moving close.

XI

Checks

When a character attempts something uncertain where failure matters, the GM calls for a check.

A check normally consists of a card value plus the appropriate attribute modifier.

Check Total
Card Value + Attribute Modifier

Classically, a check resolves with a "Meets it, Beats it" resolution.

  • An enemies armor class is 8, so a Might check resulting in an 8 meets the requirement to hit.

The GM determines the Difficulty of the check based on the circumstances.

Advantage and Disadvantage

Some circumstances may add favorable or unfortunate circumstances for characters.

For Skill checks and Saving throws, two cards are drawn and played from the top of the deck.

For actions in combat, one card is played from your hand and another is played from the top of the deck.

Advantage: From the two cards played,take the MOST favorable result. This can cause a face card effect to trigger or suit resonance to be applied.

Disadvantage: From the two cards played, take the LEAST favorable result. This can cause losing a face card effect or losing suit resonance being applied.

XII

Saving Throws

Saving Throws can be called for during normal social, explorations, and during combat when a player encouters a spell or trap. For saving throws, always draw from the top of the deck.

A Saving throw check uses the value of the top card of your deck and the relevant primary attribute value.

Some feats and abilities may increase the saving throw checks of a character.

XII

Skills

Skill checks can be called for during normal social encounters or even during combat when a player asked to do something creative. For skill checks, always draw from the top of the deck.

Training Procedure
Untrained Disadvantage on the skill check. Draw 2 cards and play 2 cards using the lower of the two values. Then add the relevant skill attribute and any other modifiers.
Proficient Draw 1 card and play one card. Then add the relevant skill attribute and any other modifiers.
Expertise Advantage on the skill check. Draw 2 cards and play 2 cards using the higher of the two values. Then add the relevant skill attribute and any other modifiers.
Skill Cards: All cards used during skill checks are placed in the Shuffle Pile.
New Skills: The skill list is not finalized and may include adding: Profession, Craft(type), Fishing, Stealth, Sleight of Hand, and Initiative

VIII

Weapons & Suit Affinity

Weapons are divided into four broad categories associated with the four suits. If you have proficiency in that weapon group, then you can use those weapons. If you don't have proficiency, then you can't use that weapon.

Suit Weapon Theme Example
♠ Spades Melee / martial weapons Swords
♥ Hearts Melee / Supportive / ceremonial weapons Maces
♣ Clubs Melee / Agile / precision weapons Daggers, Short Swords
♦ Diamonds Technical / magical weapons Wands
Ranged Weapons Specialized Training Longbows - ♠, Crossbows and Daggers - ♣, Pistols - ♦
Weapon Resonance

When you are proficient with a certain suit weapons, you gain resonance with all cards of that suit doing an additional +1 damage.

Weapon Resonance applies to all 4 suits and styles of weapons.

Weapon Mastery

During Character Creation, if there are two sources of gaining proficiency with a weapon type, gain the associated Weapon Mastery instead.

XIV

Combat Summary

Starting Combat

When combat begins, each combatant determines Initiative.

"Flip for Initiative"

Play the top card of your deck and add your Dexterity modifier.

Initiative = Card Value + Dexterity

Players win ties against enemies.

Initiative cards are placed into the Shuffle Pile after Initiative has been determined.

Acceleration

If the Initiative card is a face card, the character may burn the face card, and they gain Acceleration. The player draws one additional card at the beginning of their first turn.

The GM is to determine initiative for all characters in the scene and then the individual with the highest initiative takes their turn. When their move is complete and they have used all the cards they wish to in their hand, then play procedes to the next character in initiative order.

A round is when all characters have taken their turn from the top to the bottom of the initiative order.

Surprise and Ambush

Occasionally, players will surprise or ambush enemies and vice versa as some clever enemies may set traps for players. In this case, the surprised individuals flip for Initiative at Disadvantage.

Defense and Armor

Armor Class (AC) represents how difficult a character is to hit.

Armor AC
Unarmored 5 + Dexterity
Light Armor 6 + Dexterity
Medium Armor 7
Heavy Armor 8 or 9
Shield +1 AC

Armor that does not list Dexterity does not add the character's Dexterity modifier to AC.

Examples

  • Unarmored character with Dexterity +0: AC 5
  • Unarmored character with Dexterity +3: AC 8
  • Light Armor with Dexterity +3: AC 9
  • Medium Armor with Shield: AC 8
  • Heavy Armor with Shield: AC 10

Basic Combat Actions

Attack Total
Card Value + Relevant Attribute Modifier

If the Attack Total equals or exceeds the target's Armor Class (AC), the attack hits.

Face cards have a value of 10 and may activate special effects.

Might Strike

Action — Play 1 card.

Make a Might attack with a weapon you are proficient with in your hand. On a hit, deal 1 HP damage.

If using a face card, burn the face card, and do 2 HP of damage instead.

If you have weapon mastery, and the played card matches the suit affinity of the weapon, deal +1 HP of damage.

Ranged Strike

Action — Play 1 card.

Make a ranged attack with a weapon you are proficient with in your hand. On a hit, deal 1 HP damage.

If using a face card, burn the face card, and do 2 HP of damage instead.

If you have weapon mastery, and the played card matches the suit affinity of the weapon, deal +1 HP of damage.

Defend

Action — Play 1 card.

Add the value of the played card to your AC until the beginning of your next turn.

Shield Bash

Action — Play a Spade.

If you are proficient with shields and holding a shield, make an attack using your shield by playing a Spade card.

On a hit, deal 1 HP damage and choose to either push the target back from close to near or knock it prone.

A player may burn a face card Spade to cause the target to be pushed back and knocked prone.

Rallying Cry

Action — Play a Heart.

One ally may draw one card.

A player may burn a face card Heart to cause two allies to each draw one card or one ally to draw 2 cards.

Arcane Insight

Action — Play a Diamond.

Reveal a weakness, vulnerability, or useful insight about an enemy.

A player may burn a face card Diamond and choose two enemies to reveal information about.

Reactions

A Reaction may be used outside of your normal turn when its trigger occurs.

A card must be played to trigger a Reaction unless the ability specifically says otherwise.

Parry

Reaction — Play a card from your hand.

When you are hit by a physical attack, play a card to negate 1 HP of damage from that attack.

A player may burn a face card and negate all incoming damage from a single physical source.

Parry cannot negate spell or elemental damage.

XV

Magic

Magic is divided into four resonant domains. Detailed spellcasting rules spell lists are maintained on the dedicated Magic page.

Domain Suit Theme
Arcane ♦ Knowledge, elements, force, magical manipulation.
Divine ♥ Healing, protection, blessings, judgment.
Primal ♣ Nature, beasts, storms, transformation.
Occult ♠ Secrets, spirits, curses, shadows, forbidden power.
Spell Resource: Cards used to cast spells are placed in the Burn Pile.

Spell Tiers

Spells are divided into Tiers 0 through 5.

Tier Base Casting Cost
0 Free outside combat; any card may be Burned in combat to play a Tier 0 Spell.
1 Burn 1 card of the Resonant Suit
2 Burn 2 cards of the Resonant Suit
3 Burn 3 cards of the Resonant Suit
4 Burn 4 cards of the Resonant Suit
5 Burn 5 cards of the Resonant Suit

Magic Surge

When paying the cost to cast a spell, if a resonant face card is burned, it counts as 2 cards towards the casting of the spell.

A face card out of resonance may be burned when paying the spell cost, but it will count as one card towards the cost of casting the spell.

Examples:

A Tier 2 Arcane spell requires two cards. The first card provides its normal value to the magic attack. If the second card is a Diamond, it also adds +1 to the check because it resonates with Arcane magic.

A Tier 1 Spell Firebolt has a cost of 1 Diamond. Playing an 8 of Diamonds allows the wizard to cast firebolt and hit the enemy for 1hp of damage.

A Tier 1 Spell Firebolt has a cost of 1 Diamond. Playing an King of Spades allows the wizard to cast firebolt and hit the enemy for 1hp of damage.

A Tier 1 Spell Firebolt has a cost of 1 Diamond. Playing an 8 of diamonds allows the wizard to cast firebolt and hit the enemy for 1hp of damage. The Spell has the channel keyword, so the player burns an additional 2 of Diamonds to add +1 to the attack and to do 1 additional damage.

A Tier 1 Spell Firebolt has a cost of 1 Diamond. Playing an Jack of diamonds paying the cost of 2 diamonds to cast the spell. This allows the wizard to cast Firebolt and hit the enemy for 2hp of damage due to the channel keyword.

A Tier 2 Arcane Burst has a cost requiring 2 Diamonds. Playing an King of diamonds is paying the cost of 2 diamonds to cast the spell. This allows the wizard to cast Arcane Burst and hit the enemy for 3hp of damage, due to the channel keyword the player can burn a 5 of Diamonds doing 4 hp of damage.

Magic in Combat

Casting a spell in combat requires the appropriate number of cards to be Burned to cast the spell.

The first card Burned for the spell contributes its card value to the magic attack.

Each additional card that matches the spell's resonant suit adds +1 to the magic attack.

Magic Outside Combat

When casting a spell outside combat, the caster may search their deck for the cards needed to pay the spell's cost, provided the cards match the spell's resonant suit. Tier 0 spells, do not require a card to be selected.

The selected cards are then Burned.

Channel

Some spells have the Channel property.

After paying the spell's normal casting cost, the caster may Burn additional cards to strengthen the spell.

When a Channel effect specifies a resonant suit, each appropriate additional card increases the spell's effect as described by that spell.

Spell Scrolls

Some spells have been magically inscribed on parchment for later use.

If the spell is known by the caster, or on the Caster's Spell list, then they can use the spell scroll. Otherwise, the contents of the scroll appear as gibberish.

In the creation of a spell scroll, the casting cost has already been paid. Any card may be discarded as a magic attack to determine if the spell hits. Hit or miss the scroll turns to dust.

Spell cast from Spell Scrolls cannot benefit from the channel property.

Concentration Spells

Some spells are powerful and require a constant focus to maintain their effect.

Only one concentration spell can be in effect by a character at a time.

If a player takes damage in combat or some other effect as determined by the GM may affect spell concentration, the player loses concentration unless they burn the top card of their deck.

XVI

Gobble, Food, and Potions

When a player has an Ace in their hand, they may use the Gobble action.

Gobble

Special Action — Burn an Ace.

Burn an Ace and consume a food, drink, potion, or other consumable item.

Apply the item's effect.

Persistent Food Effects

Only one persistent food effect may be active at a time.

Consuming a new food item with a persistent effect removes the previous persistent food effect and replaces it with the new one.

Example

A fighter consumes a Protein Bar and gains +1 Might until their next Long Rest. Later, they consume a Fizzy Drink. The +1 Might effect ends and is replaced by +1 Dexterity.

Example Consumables

Item Effect
Bread Recover 1 HP.
Health Potion Recover 3 HP.
Protein Bar +1 Might until your next Long Rest.
Fizzy Drink +1 Dexterity until your next Long Rest.
Arcane Tea +1 Cunning until your next Long Rest.
Comfort Stew +1 Spirit until your next Long Rest.
Trail Mix Recover 1 HP and gain +1 Survival until your next Long Rest.
Herbal Tonic Recover 2 HP.
Ironbrew +1 AC until your next Long Rest.
Swiftroot Juice Increase your movement by 1 until your next Long Rest.
XVII

Hit Points and Defeat

Characters and enemies have Hit Points (HP).

Reaching 0 HP

When a player character reaches 0 HP, they fall unconscious and fall to the ground.

An unconscious character cannot fight or take normal actions.

Recovering Consciousness

Whenever an unconscious player character gains HP from any source, they immediately regain consciousness and may fight and act normally.

Unconscious Consumption Only liquid drinks and potions may be given to another player to assist in recovering their hit points.

Party Defeat

If the entire party is unconscious, the DM determines the fate of the party.

This may result in capture, rescue, retreat, consequences from the encounter, or another outcome appropriate to the adventure.

XVIII

Rest

Short Rest

A Short Rest is a period of recovery during an adventure. Class abilities and other features that specify Short Rest recovery become available again.

  • Shuffle the Discard Pile into the Deck.
  • Take 1 Guzzle Action if desired.
  • Cast Spells following the out of combat spell casting rules.

Long Rest

A Long Rest represents substantial recovery between adventures or after a safe period of downtime.

Recover all HP and shuffle all cards from the Burn and Shuffle piles back into the deck.

XIX

Character Creation

  1. Choose an Ancestry.
  2. Choose two Ancestry Feats available to that Ancestry.
  3. Choose a Background.
  4. Apply the Background's skill proficiencies and minor ability.
  5. Choose a Core Class.
  6. Apply the class's Level 1 features.
  7. Determine your core attributes.
  8. Choose starting equipment.
  9. Begin your Adventure
Starting Attributes
Standard Array: -1,0,+1,+2
Allocation: All Attributes start at -1. You have 6 points to allocate to each of the attributes not to exceed +2.
GM Discretion: Your GM may have their own preferred way to determine starting core attributes.
XX

Character Advancement

Level Advancement
1 Gain class identity features. Starting Hand Size: 2.
2 Gain a subclass feature.
3 Gain a class identity feature. Hand Size increases to 3.
4 Gain a subclass feature.
5 Increase one core attribute by +1. Hand Size increases to 4.

After reaching Level 5, characters continue to develop through Feat Points rather than gaining additional character levels.

XXI

The Spirit of the Rules

Low Math

Most resolutions should require little more than adding a card value and an attribute modifier.

Meaningful Choices

Spending a strong card now should matter because that card will not be immediately available later.

Asymmetric Characters

Characters of the same class should be able to feel substantially different through feats and subclasses.

Heroic Fantasy

Characters should feel capable and powerful while still facing meaningful danger in combat and dungeon exploration.

Realm and Rune is intended to be lightweight and fast. Players should have meaningful choices without requiring extensive bookkeeping.

The card system is designed to reward tactical decisions, resource management, and creative use of abilities.

When a rule is unclear, the DM should make a reasonable ruling and keep the game moving. Rules can be clarified or changed after the session as part of the playtest process.

Playtest Principle

If a rule repeatedly slows down play, causes confusion, or prevents players from doing something fun, record the problem and revisit the rule after the session.
