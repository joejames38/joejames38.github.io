1. Game Concept

Hero Deck is a lightweight heroic fantasy tabletop roleplaying game that uses standard playing cards instead of dice.

Each player controls a personal deck. Cards represent a hero's skill, preparation, fortune, endurance, and heroic potential.

The Core Choice:

Spend powerful cards now to overcome immediate danger, or preserve them for a greater challenge later.

Cards have two primary purposes:

  • Card value helps determine whether an action succeeds.
  • Card suit activates class abilities, weapon effects, and other special features.

2. Core Resolution

Basic Test

Card Value + Relevant Attribute Modifier ≥ Difficulty

If the final result equals or exceeds the Difficulty, the action succeeds.

The Game Master determines whether an action requires a test. Routine actions should succeed automatically.

Suggested Difficulty Scale

Difficulty Target Description
Easy 5 Requires basic competence.
Standard 8 A meaningful challenge.
Difficult 10 Requires skill or good fortune.
Heroic 12 Exceptional effort.
Legendary 14+ Extraordinary achievement.
Playtest Note:

Difficulty values are provisional. The final difficulty scale must be tested against the card distribution, attribute range, and proficiency system.

3. Cards and Decks

Each player uses a personal standard playing-card deck. The starting deck composition is not yet finalized.

Card Value Effect
2–10 Printed value Normal action resolution.
Jack 10 Triggers enhanced face-card effects.
Queen 10 Triggers enhanced face-card effects.
King 10 Triggers enhanced face-card effects.
Ace — Snake Eyes: Automatic failure with no additional consequence.
Joker Optional Critical fumble or major complication.

Face Cards

All face cards have a numerical value of 10. When a face card is used with an ability that includes a Face Card effect, the enhanced effect is activated.

Face Card Rule:

A Jack, Queen, or King always counts as a value of 10 and may activate an ability's enhanced effect.

Aces — Snake Eyes

When an Ace is drawn to resolve an action, the action automatically fails.

Snake Eyes does not automatically create an additional penalty or complication.

Unresolved:

The interaction between Aces and cards held in a player's hand still requires a final rule.

Optional Jokers

Jokers are an optional rule and are not part of the core playtest.

If used, a Joker may represent a critical fumble, unexpected disaster, or major complication.

Card Zones

Zone Purpose
Deck Cards available to draw.
Hand Cards currently available to play.
Shuffle Pile Played number cards. These return when the deck is depleted.
Discard Pile Played face cards. These return during a rest.

Card Recovery

  • Played number cards enter the Shuffle Pile.
  • Played face cards enter the Discard Pile.
  • When the Deck is empty, shuffle the Shuffle Pile to create a new Deck.
  • During a Short Rest, shuffle all cards into the Deck.
  • During a Long Rest, recover HP and shuffle all cards into the Deck.
Playtest Question:

Does separating number cards and face cards create enough resource pressure without causing excessive deck management?

4. Core Attributes

Characters have four core attributes. Each attribute is associated with a playing-card suit.

Attribute Suit Areas of Expertise
Might ♠ Spades Strength, endurance, physical power, and melee combat.
Spirit ♥ Hearts Courage, leadership, healing, emotion, and social influence.
Dexterity ♣ Clubs Agility, precision, speed, risk, and ranged combat.
Cunning ♦ Diamonds Knowledge, quick thinking, investigation, and magical prowess.

Attributes normally range from −1 to +3.

Suggested Starting Array

+2, +1, 0, −1

Modifier Description
−1 A notable weakness.
0 Ordinary or untrained.
+1 Competent.
+2 Highly skilled.
+3 Exceptional or heroic.

5. Scenes and Turns

A scene is a period of focused play involving meaningful danger, conflict, exploration, social interaction, or another structured challenge.

Beginning a Scene

At the beginning of a scene, draw cards until you reach your maximum Hand Size.

Beginning Your Turn

At the beginning of your turn, draw one card.

Turn Economy

Each turn provides:

  • One Move: Movement does not require a card.
  • Card Actions: Each card played may activate one Action.

A character may play multiple cards and perform multiple Actions during the same turn.

A player may spend their entire hand during a single turn, or preserve cards for later turns and reactions.

Explosive Play:

A character may unleash several actions in one turn if they have cards available.

This creates a tactical choice between immediate power and future flexibility.

End of Turn

If a character exceeds their maximum Hand Size, they discard cards until they are at their limit.

Playtest Question:

Should the player choose discarded cards, or should excess cards be discarded randomly?

Current draft: the player chooses.

6. Actions and Reactions

A card must be played to activate an Action or Reaction.

An Action normally occurs during the character's turn. A Reaction occurs when its trigger is met and may occur outside the character's turn.

Movement

Each character receives one Move per turn. Movement does not require a card.

Rules Needed:
  • Movement distance
  • Grid versus theater-of-the-mind movement
  • Terrain effects
  • Engagement and opportunity attacks

7. Combat

Combat uses the same core card-resolution system as other challenges.

Attack Card Value + Relevant Attribute Modifier ≥ Target AC

On a successful attack, the target takes the damage specified by the Action or weapon.

Basic Actions

Might Strike

Action Might

Play one card and make a Might attack using a melee weapon you are holding.

Hit: Deal 1 HP damage.

Matching Weapon Suit: If the card played matches the weapon's suit, deal 2 HP damage instead.

Ranged Strike

Action Dexterity

Play one card and make a Dexterity attack using a ranged weapon you are holding.

Hit: Deal 1 HP damage.

Matching Weapon Suit: If the card played matches the weapon's suit, deal 2 HP damage instead.

Defend

Action

Discard a card to increase your AC until your next turn.

Unresolved:

The exact AC bonus is not finalized.

Possible approaches:

  • Add the full card value to AC against one attack.
  • Add half the card value to AC until the next turn.
  • Use fixed bonuses based on card type.

Dodge

Reaction

When an attack hits you, play or discard one card to reduce the damage by 1 HP.

Dodge provides a useful way to spend low-value cards and manage the hand.

Rules Needed:

Clarify whether Dodge works against:

  • Physical attacks
  • Ranged attacks
  • Spells
  • Environmental damage

Example Suit Abilities

Shield Bash

Action Spade

Play a Spade and make a Might attack.

Hit: Deal 1 HP damage and choose one: push the target back or knock it prone.

Face Card: Push the target back and knock it prone.

Rallying Cry

Action Heart

Play a Heart. One ally draws one card.

Face Card: Choose up to two allies. Each draws one card.

Backstab

Action Club

Play a Club and make a Dexterity attack.

Hit: Deal 2 HP damage.

Face Card: Deal 3 HP damage instead.

Rules Needed:

Define the conditions required for Backstab:

  • Target is flanked
  • Target is distracted
  • Target is unaware
  • Target is adjacent to an ally

Arcane Insight

Action Diamond

Play a Diamond and learn one useful weakness, resistance, vulnerability, tactic, or hidden trait of an enemy.

Face Card: Choose up to two enemies.

8. Weapons

Weapons belong to one of the four suit categories. Any character may use any weapon.

When the card used for an attack matches the weapon's suit, the weapon gains an additional damage point or another unique effect.

Suit Theme Possible Weapons Possible Effects
♠ Spades Power and martial force Swords, axes, heavy weapons Extra damage, slow, push, or armor penetration
♥ Hearts Protection and leadership Maces, shields, holy weapons Guard allies, heal, rally, or protect
♣ Clubs Precision and mobility Daggers, bows, finesse weapons Mobility, bleed, or tactical positioning
♦ Diamonds Magic and knowledge Wands, staves, runeblades Elemental effects, spells, or magical control
Playtest Question:

Should matching a weapon's suit always provide +1 damage, or should each weapon have a unique perk?

9. Skills

Skills represent specialized training. Each skill is associated with a core attribute.

Skill Proficiency

Training Skill Check
Untrained Draw two cards and use the lower value. Add the relevant Attribute Modifier.
Proficient Draw one card and play one card from your Hand. Add the relevant Attribute Modifier.
Expert Draw one card and play up to two cards from your Hand. Add the relevant Attribute Modifier.
Major Rules Gap:

The method for combining multiple cards is not yet defined.

The current draft says that proficient characters draw one card and play one card, while experts may play up to two cards.

The game must define whether those cards:

  • Are added together
  • Allow the player to choose the highest
  • Represent separate parts of the action
  • Provide bonuses or special effects

This is a high-priority playtest decision.

10. Character Creation

Create a character using the following steps.

  1. Choose an Ancestry.
  2. Choose two Ancestry Feats.
  3. Choose a Background.
  4. Gain two Background Skill Proficiencies.
  5. Choose a Class.
  6. Assign Attribute Modifiers.
  7. Choose starting equipment.
  8. Prepare the starting deck.

Starting equipment, starting HP, and starting deck composition are not yet finalized.

14. Subclasses

Each class will eventually include one or more subclasses.

Each subclass contains a pool of 10 independent features.

Subclass features are not locked into a fixed order.

Characters choose one feature at:

  • Level 2
  • Level 4
  • Level 6

After Level 6, additional subclass features may be acquired using Feat Points.

This allows two characters with the same class and subclass to develop differently.

Future Development:

Subclass feature pools have intentionally not been designed yet.

15. Advancement

The maximum character level is Level 6.

After reaching Level 6, characters continue to grow through Feat Points.

Feat Points may purchase:

  • Additional subclass features
  • New combat abilities
  • Skill expertise
  • Additional ancestry feats
  • Weapon mastery
  • Card manipulation abilities
  • Other advanced options
Rules Needed:
  • How many Feat Points are awarded?
  • When are Feat Points awarded?
  • How much does each feat cost?
  • Can Feat Points improve attributes?
  • Can characters purchase features from other classes?

16. Playtest Questions

Card Economy

  • Is a starting Hand Size of 2 large enough?
  • Does drawing one card each turn provide enough actions?
  • Do players regularly empty their hands?
  • Do players hoard face cards?
  • Are face cards too rare or too common?
  • Does separating face cards into the Discard Pile create meaningful resource pressure?
  • How often does a player's Deck become empty?

Action Economy

  • Do multiple card actions create exciting turns?
  • Can one character dominate a scene by spending their entire hand?
  • Are players encouraged to preserve cards for Reactions?
  • Should any Actions be limited to once per turn?

Combat

  • How much HP should characters have?
  • How much HP should common enemies have?
  • How quickly should combat end?
  • Are matching weapon effects meaningful?
  • Are low-value cards useful enough?
  • Does Dodge provide sufficient value for low cards?

Skills

  • Is the untrained penalty too severe?
  • Does proficiency feel valuable?
  • Is expertise too powerful?
  • How should multiple cards be combined?
  • Should skill checks consume cards in the same way as combat Actions?

Character Progression

  • Does Hand Size growth feel meaningful?
  • Does Level 6 provide enough advancement?
  • Do Feat Points provide long-term growth?
  • Should the deck grow as characters level?

18. Recommended Development Roadmap

Phase 1 — Core Math

  1. Define how multiple skill cards combine.
  2. Set target Difficulty values.
  3. Set AC values.
  4. Set starting HP and enemy HP.
  5. Test attribute modifiers.

Phase 2 — Combat Prototype

  1. Create three player characters.
  2. Create three enemy types.
  3. Define initiative.
  4. Define movement.
  5. Define conditions.
  6. Run short combat encounters.

Phase 3 — Card Economy Testing

  1. Test a full 52-card deck.
  2. Test a number-only starting deck.
  3. Test class-specific face cards.
  4. Measure card use per encounter.
  5. Measure face-card use.
  6. Measure deck depletion.

Phase 4 — Character Options

  1. Finalize ancestry feats.
  2. Finalize background features.
  3. Design one subclass for each class.
  4. Create the first feat catalog.

Phase 5 — Adventure Playtest

  1. Create a small dungeon.
  2. Include combat.
  3. Include traps.
  4. Include exploration.
  5. Include a social encounter.
  6. Include a boss encounter.
  7. Run a complete Level 1 adventure.
