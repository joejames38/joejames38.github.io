Realm & Rune
Class Rules
I

Classes

Your class defines your character's primary role, abilities, and approach to adventure. Classes provide the strongest mechanical identity for a character.

Realm & Rune has four core classes: Fighter, Rogue, Cleric, and Wizard.

Maximum Level: 5

Characters advance from Level 1 through Level 5. Class features are gained at each level, while subclass features provide additional opportunities for asymmetrical character development.

II

Level Advancement

Every character follows the same basic level progression, although the actual abilities gained at each level differ significantly between classes.

Level Class Progression
1 Gain class identity features. Starting Hand Size: 2.
2 Gain a subclass feature.
3 Gain a class identity feature. Hand Size increases to 3.
4 Gain a subclass feature.
5 Increase one Core Attribute by +1. Hand Size increases to 4.
Beyond Level 5

Level advancement ends at Level 5. Further character development comes through Feat Points, which can be spent to acquire additional abilities.

III

Subclasses

Each core class contains multiple subclasses. A character chooses a subclass and gains subclass features at Levels 2 and 4.

Subclasses are intentionally designed to support asymmetrical growth. Two characters can share the same class and subclass while still developing very differently.

Subclass Feat Design

Each subclass will eventually contain approximately 10 independent features.

These features are not locked to specific levels. When a character gains a subclass feature, they select an available feature from their subclass.

This means two Fighters of the same subclass may select completely different combinations of subclass features, creating distinct characters without requiring a large number of separate subclasses.

IV

Fighter

Fighters are masters of weapons and armor. They excel at direct confrontation and are capable of adapting their tactics to almost any battlefield.

Class Identity

The Fighter is the most broadly capable martial class. Fighters have access to every weapon and armor category and gain powerful ways to manipulate their card resources during combat.

Level Progression

Level Features
1 Martial Mastery
  • Gain Proficiency with all weapons.
  • Gain Proficiency with all armor.
  • Gain Proficiency with shields.
  • Gain Proficiency in any 2 skills.
  • Gain one Weapon Mastery Feat.
Second Wind

Once per Short Rest, discard a card to recover 1 HP.

2 Gain one Subclass Feature.
3 Action Surge

Once per Short Rest, discard any face card and draw cards until you reach your current hand size.

4 Gain one Subclass Feature.
5 Attribute Increase

Increase one Core Attribute by +1.

Hand Size increases to 4.

V

Rogue

Rogues rely on speed, precision, deception, and opportunistic attacks. They are highly skilled characters capable of excelling in and out of combat.

Class Identity

The Rogue specializes in skill checks, mobility, stealth, and precise attacks. Rather than overpowering an enemy, the Rogue looks for the right moment to strike.

Level Progression

Level Features
1 Roguish Training
  • Gain Proficiency with Club weapons.
  • Gain Proficiency with Light Armor.
  • Gain Proficiency in any 3 skills.
  • Gain Expertise in 1 skill.
Backstab

Play a Club card to make a Backstab attack. On a hit, deal 2 HP damage.

If the card played is a face card, deal an additional 1 HP damage.

2 Gain one Subclass Feature.
3 Hide

Discard a card to Hide.

While Hidden, the Rogue can take advantage of opportunities to make attacks or perform actions from concealment as determined by the situation.

4 Gain one Subclass Feature.
5 Attribute Increase

Increase one Core Attribute by +1.

Hand Size increases to 4.

VI

Cleric

Clerics wield Divine magic through faith, conviction, and supernatural power. They combine martial capability with healing and magical support.

Class Identity

The Cleric is the primary Divine spellcasting class. Clerics gain access to the full Divine spell list as they advance and can support allies while remaining capable combatants.

Level Progression

Level Features
1 Divine Training
  • Gain Proficiency with Heart weapons.
  • Gain Proficiency with Light Armor.
  • Gain Proficiency with Medium Armor.
  • Gain Proficiency with shields.
  • Gain Proficiency in any 2 skills.
  • Gain access to all Tier 1 Divine Spells.
2 Gain one Subclass Feature.
3 Divine Advancement

Gain access to all Tier 2 Divine Spells.

4 Gain one Subclass Feature.
5 Divine Mastery

Increase one Core Attribute by +1.

Gain access to all Tier 3 Divine Spells.

Hand Size increases to 4.

VII

Wizard

Wizards are dedicated students of Arcane magic. They possess the broadest ability to learn and customize their spell selection, making them highly adaptable magical specialists.

Class Identity

Wizards are flexible Arcane spellcasters. Unlike Clerics, who automatically gain access to entire tiers of Divine magic, Wizards build a personal spell library by learning individual spells.

Level Progression

Level Features
1 Arcane Training
  • Gain Proficiency in any 2 skills.
  • Learn 3 Tier 1 Arcane Spells.
2 Arcane Study

Gain one Subclass Feature.

Learn 1 additional Tier 1 Arcane Spell.

3 Advanced Arcana

Gain access to all Tier 2 Arcane Spells.

Learn 1 additional Arcane Spell.

4 Arcane Study

Gain one Subclass Feature.

Learn 1 Tier 1 or Tier 2 Arcane Spell.

5 Arcane Mastery

Increase one Core Attribute by +1.

Learn 1 Tier 1, Tier 2, or Tier 3 Arcane Spell.

Hand Size increases to 4.

IX

Class Design Philosophy

The four classes are intentionally asymmetrical. They should not simply be four different ways of performing the same actions.

  • Fighter should excel at direct combat, equipment flexibility, and recovering combat resources.
  • Rogue should excel at skills, Expertise, positioning, stealth, and high-value attacks.
  • Cleric should excel at healing, support, protection, and reliable access to Divine magic.
  • Wizard should excel at magical flexibility, spell selection, and adapting their capabilities to the situation.
Asymmetrical Growth

Class balance does not require every class to receive the same number or type of abilities. Instead, each class should have a distinct way of converting cards into meaningful actions.

X

Classes and the Card Economy

Every class uses the same fundamental card economy, but class features manipulate that economy differently.

Class Card Economy Identity
Fighter Can recover cards and convert face cards into additional opportunities through Action Surge.
Rogue Uses cards for precise attacks and skill specialization.
Cleric Converts Hearts into healing, protection, and Divine magic.
Wizard Burns cards as a limited magical resource and benefits strongly from managing Resonant suits.
XI

Class Playtest Questions

  • Does each class feel meaningfully different after only one or two sessions?
  • Is Fighter's universal weapon and armor proficiency too broad compared with the other classes?
  • Does Action Surge create enough excitement to justify requiring a face card?
  • Does Rogue's Expertise create a sufficiently noticeable advantage over normal proficiency?
  • Does Hide require additional rules for defining when a character is Hidden?
  • Is Cleric's automatic access to entire spell tiers too powerful compared with the Wizard's individual spell learning?
  • Should Wizards eventually gain access to Tier 4 and Tier 5 spells through later Feat Points rather than normal class progression?
  • Are the four classes sufficiently distinct without subclasses?
  • Do subclass features provide enough room for two characters of the same class to develop differently?
  • Does the Level 5 cap provide enough class progression before characters transition entirely to Feat Points?
