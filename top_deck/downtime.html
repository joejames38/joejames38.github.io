Realm & Rune
A Card-Driven Fantasy Roleplaying Game

Downtime

Rest, recover, work, explore, and turn time between adventures into useful resources.

Downtime at a Glance

Downtime usually takes half a day or a full day. A character may choose one primary downtime activity during that period. The GM may shorten, extend, or combine activities when appropriate.

Downtime is intentionally flexible: use it to gain resources, develop relationships, gather information, and create adventure hooks.

1. Fishing 🎣

Time: 1 hour or more · Skill: Survival · Requires fishing gear and suitable water

Draw two cards face-up as the boundaries of your catch. Draw a third card known as the Catch Card. If its value falls between the first two, you catch something. Aces count as 1; face cards count as 10.

After seeing the two initial cards, the player chooses what kind of cast they want to do:

Safe Cast: Fish normally.
Risky Cast: +1 to the catch card, but also +1 for a better catch.
Deep Cast: +2 to the catch card, but also a +2 for a better catch, but risks loosing their hook or damaging their fishing gear requiring fishing to end for the day.

Survival Proficiency: Once per attempt, redraw the catch card. Expertise: redraw twice.

If the player successfully catches a fish, draw a final card to determine what they've reeled in!

CardCatch
2–3Small Fish — Recover 1 HP when prepared.
4–5Medium Fish — Recover 2 HP when prepared.
6–7Large Fish — Can provide a +1 Might food effect.
8–9Rare Fish — Can provide a +1 Dexterity food effect.
10Trophy Fish — Valuable or unusually potent food.
JMagical Fish — Can provide a +1 Cunning food effect.
QExotic Fish — Can provide a +1 Spirit food effect.
KLegendary Fish — Rare ingredient, treasure, or powerful food.
ASomething Else... Trigger a strange discovery or encounter.

2. Cook & Prepare Food 🍲

Time: 1–4 hours · No skill required

Anyone can cook. During downtime, a character may turn ingredients into prepared food using a simple card-based cooking game. The quality of the finished food depends on both the cooking result and the ingredients used.

The Cooking Game

  1. Choose the ingredients. The player tells the GM what ingredients they are using. The GM determines the possible food effects based on those ingredients.
  2. Draw two cards. Add their values together. Aces may be counted as either 1 or 11. Face cards are worth 10.
  3. Keep cooking or stop. The player may draw another card and add its value to the current total. They may continue drawing cards as long as they wish.
  4. Do not go over 21. If the total exceeds 21, the cooking attempt is a failure and the ingredients are ruined.

Cooking Results

Total Result
16 or lower Simple Success. The food is successfully prepared and provides its normal effect.
17–20 Great Success. The food is exceptionally well prepared. Improve or strengthen the normal effect of the ingredients.
21 Wondrous Success. The food is extraordinary. In addition to its normal effect, it gains a special food effect appropriate to the ingredients.
22+ Burned. The food is ruined and the ingredients are lost.

Ingredient Effects

The cooking result does not determine what the food does. Instead, the ingredients determine the food's basic effect, while the quality of the cooking determines how strong that effect is.

The GM should use the existing Food & Gobble rules when determining appropriate effects. Cooking should not normally create effects that are significantly more powerful than other food available in the game.

Example: Cooking a Fish

A character has caught a Large Fish while fishing. Large Fish can be used to create a food that grants +1 Might until the next Long Rest.

The player begins cooking and draws: 7 + 5 = 12. They decide to keep cooking and draw a 4, bringing the total to 16.

The player could stop at 16 for a Simple Success, but decides to risk another card. They draw a 3, bringing the total to 19.

The result is a Great Success. The fish is prepared exceptionally well, so the GM might allow the resulting meal to grant +1 Might until the next Long Rest and recover 1 HP.

If the player had instead reached exactly 21, the meal would be a Wondrous Success. The GM could allow the fish to grant its normal +1 Might effect, recover 2 HP, and provide an additional minor benefit appropriate to the fish.

Cooking is a push-your-luck game. A safe result can produce a perfectly good meal, while continuing to draw cards creates the possibility of a much better meal at the risk of burning all the ingredients.

Persistent food rule: Only one persistent food effect can be active at a time. Consuming a new food with a persistent effect replaces the previous persistent food effect.

3. Train & Practice ⚔

Time: Half day or full day · Skill: Varies

Practice a weapon, technique, language, craft, or other established capability.

Choose a reasonable area of training and make an appropriate check or work with a trainer. Success establishes progress toward a future proficiency, Feat Point opportunity, or narrative benefit.

Training should not bypass character-level requirements or provide unlimited permanent advancement.

4. Work for Coin 🪙

Time: One full day · Skill: Appropriate skill

A character can spend a day working a temporary job to earn money. The character chooses an appropriate skill for the work they are performing, then makes a normal skill check against the downtime work DC.

The exact job can be determined by the player or GM. Examples include guarding a caravan, working in a tavern, repairing equipment, helping craftsmen, hunting, hauling cargo, performing, translating, or assisting a local business.

Work Results

Skill Check Result Pay
7 or lower Minor Success Draw 1 card. Gain that many silver pieces.
8–9 Good Wage Draw 3 cards and add their values together. Gain that many silver pieces.
10+ Unexpected Boon Draw 1 card. Gain that many gold pieces.

Aces count as 1 and face cards count as 10 when determining wages.

Example Jobs

  • Guard Work: Athletics, Perception, or Intimidation
  • Tavern Work: Performance, Persuasion, or Athletics
  • Craft Work: Investigation, History, or an appropriate background skill
  • Hunting: Survival or Animal Handling
  • Research Assistant: Arcana, History, or Investigation
  • Street Work: Thievery, Deception, or Stealth

Example

A rogue spends a day working as a lookout for a merchant caravan. The GM calls for a Perception check. The result is 9, producing a Good Wage.

The player draws three cards: 4, 7, and Queen. The Queen counts as 10, producing a total of 21 silver pieces for the day's work.

If the rogue had instead rolled a 10 or higher, they would receive an Unexpected Boon. Drawing a 6 would result in 6 gold pieces, potentially making the day's work substantially more profitable.

5. Research 📚

Time: Half day or full day · Skill: Any appropriate knowledge skill

Research allows a character to investigate a subject, location, creature, historical event, magical phenomenon, or other mystery. The player first chooses the skill they are using to conduct the research.

Appropriate skills might include Arcana, History, Investigation, Religion/Occult, Medicine, or another skill the GM considers appropriate.

The Research Game

Research uses a modified Texas Hold'em-style card game. The player does not need to know how to play poker; they are simply looking for matching card values in the five cards revealed by the GM.

  1. Choose a skill. The player declares what skill they are using and what they are attempting to learn.
  2. Determine research cards. The player receives cards based on their proficiency: 1 card if untrained, 2 cards if proficient, or 4 cards if they have expertise.
  3. The GM deals five cards. These five cards form the research information available to the character.
  4. Combine the cards. The player's research cards are considered alongside the five cards dealt by the GM. The resulting cards are used to determine the research result.

Research Results

Hand Result
No Pair No Significant Discovery. The character finds only common or already-known information.
Pair Minor Success. The character uncovers one useful piece of information.
Two Pair Greater Success. The character uncovers several useful facts or a significant piece of information.
Three of a Kind Excellent Success. The character uncovers important or difficult-to-find information that can meaningfully affect the adventure.

What Information Is Revealed?

The degree of success determines how much information the character uncovers. The skill used determines what kind of information is revealed.

Skill Possible Information
Arcana Magical properties, spell effects, weaknesses, magical history, rituals, enchantments, or information about magical creatures.
History Historical events, important figures, old conflicts, locations of ruins, noble lineages, or forgotten connections.
Investigation Physical evidence, hidden connections, identities, motives, timelines, clues, or locations of hidden objects.
Religion/Occult Gods, cults, rituals, undead, spirits, demons, forbidden practices, symbols, or supernatural weaknesses.
Medicine Diseases, poisons, injuries, physical weaknesses, unusual anatomy, or methods of treating a condition.

Example: Researching a Goblin Warlord

The party has encountered a powerful goblin warlord and wants to learn more about him before entering his stronghold. The wizard chooses Arcana because the party suspects the warlord possesses a magical weapon. The wizard is proficient in Arcana and therefore receives 2 research cards.

The GM deals five cards and, together with the player's research cards, a pair is revealed. This is a Minor Success.

The GM might reveal that the warlord's weapon is enchanted and that its magical effects are connected to an ancient goblin ritual.

If the cards instead produced Two Pair, the GM could reveal that the weapon is enchanted, explain what kind of magic powers it, and reveal that the ritual requires a particular gemstone.

With a Three of a Kind, the GM could reveal all of the above and provide a significant tactical advantage—for example, the weapon's weakness, the location of the gemstone, and a way to disrupt the ritual before the party enters the stronghold.

Research Is Player-Directed

The player should state what they are trying to discover before the cards are dealt. This prevents a successful research result from becoming a random collection of facts and gives the GM a clear direction for determining the information revealed.

A successful research result does not necessarily answer the entire question. Instead, it should provide enough information to help the players make a decision, solve a problem, or pursue a new lead.

6. Forage 🌿

Time: Half day · Skill: Survival, Arcana, or another appropriate skill

Search the surrounding area for useful natural resources. The character chooses an appropriate skill based on what they are looking for. Survival is used for ordinary natural resources, while Arcana can be used to identify magical or unusual resources.

Foraging Mini-Game

  1. The GM plays a card. This represents the first clue about what is available in the area.
  2. The player guesses Higher or Lower. They predict whether the next card will have a higher or lower value than the GM's card.
  3. Draw the next card. If the player's prediction is correct, they gain 1 success. The new card becomes the reference card for the next prediction.
  4. Keep going. After each success, the player may continue searching and make another Higher or Lower prediction.

If the prediction is wrong, the foraging attempt ends. The character keeps any items earned from previous successes.

If the next card has the same value as the reference card, the prediction fails and the foraging attempt ends.

A player may voluntarily stop after any successful prediction and keep the items they have found.

Foraging Results

Successes Find
1 Common resource
2 Useful or uncommon resource
3 Rare resource
4 Very rare or valuable resource
5+ Exceptional discovery, magical resource, or special find

Skill Determines What Is Found

The number of successes determines the rarity of the discovery, while the skill used determines its type.

Skill Possible Finds
Survival Edible plants, berries, medicinal herbs, mushrooms, roots, game animals, useful wood, natural materials
Arcana Magical mushrooms, rare spices, alchemical ingredients, crystals, magical plants, strange minerals
Perception Hidden caches, unusual materials, nests, shed monster components, lost objects, and other overlooked discoveries
Investigation Tracks, hidden resources, abandoned supplies, clues, old campsites, and unusual locations

The GM may allow another skill when the character has a reasonable approach to the environment.

Examples

A character using Survival might find common berries after one success, medicinal herbs after two, a valuable healing plant after three, or an exceptionally rare herb after five.

A character using Arcana might find strange mushrooms after one success, unusual spices or crystals after two, a rare magical ingredient after three, or an extremely valuable alchemical component after five.

GM Guidance: The exact item should depend on the environment. A forest, swamp, mountain, dungeon, and magical region should all produce different discoveries.

7. Socialize & Network 🍺

Time: Evening or full day · No skill required

Spend time in taverns, guild halls, markets, noble houses, festivals, temples, or other social locations. Socializing uses a simple card game called Blind Man's Bet to determine how successfully the character makes connections.

Blind Man's Bet

One additional card is played to the center face-down as a non-speaking third person. They always take the In-bid.

  1. The GM draws a random card and places it face-up in the center of the table. If the card is red or a diamond or heart,then cards placed on the head. If the card black with a club or spade, you only see the back of everyone's card.
  2. The GM Deals a card to everyone face-down.Optional rule, deal an additional card to a silent non-speaking fictional person that will always take the in bid.
  3. When a red start is called, everyone picks up their card without looking at and places it on their forehead. When a black start is called, everyone looks at only their card. Optional Rule: flip the card for fictional person on the Red Start and leave it face down for a black start.
  4. After all the players have had the opportunity to see everyone else's cards or only their own, then bidding begins, starting with the player to the dealer's immediate left. Each player can make one of two possible bids being an In Bid or an Out Bid. An In bid is a bid indicating the player wants to remain in the hand thinking they have the highest card or an Out bid convinced they don't have a high card. When a player makes an Out bid, they are dropping from the hand by throwing their card face up on the table in front of everyone. The Bidding continues around the table. If all players but one drop from the hand, this last remaining player is immediately declared the winner of the hand.
  5. Reveal All Cards. The player with the highest card wins. Ace's low with a value of 1 through to king high at a value of 13.
  6. Points are scored as follows: An Out-bid is +1 point. An in bid with a loss is -3 points. An In-bid with the highest card is +5 points. If two players tie for the highest card, they both gain 3 points.
  7. Play three rounds. After three rounds, total the player's points. If a player has more points than the GM(and the optional person), they gain one success.

Play 3 rounds of 3 hands.

Social Results

Successes Result
0 Quiet Evening. The character has an uneventful evening. They may still enjoy the local scene and learn ordinary rumors.
1 Useful Conversation. The character learns a useful rumor or makes a minor acquaintance.
2 Good Connection. The character establishes a useful contact.
3 Excellent Connection. The character establishes a particularly valuable contact who can provide a meaningful favor.

Contacts

A successful socializing activity should create an actual NPC contact rather than simply granting a numerical bonus. The GM determines who the character meets based on the location and the player's interests.

City Guard: A friendly guard may overlook or help resolve a minor crime violation, provide information about the city's watch, or warn the party about trouble before it happens.

Noble: A noble contact may invite the character to a private party, social gathering, auction, or other exclusive event. They may also provide introductions to other members of high society.

Merchant: A merchant contact may provide information about rare goods, arrange access to unusual merchandise, or offer the party a better opportunity to buy or sell an item.

Guild Member: A guild contact may provide work, information, introductions, or access to guild facilities.

Tavern Regular: A local regular may know rumors, gossip, local troublemakers, or stories about nearby locations.

Example

The party is spending a night in a large city. The player wants their character to meet someone useful in the local government. The GM agrees that a City Guard is an appropriate potential contact.

Round 1: The GM's card is a 7. The player's hidden card is a 10. The player guesses Higher and scores 1 point.

Round 2: The GM's card is a Queen, worth 10. The player's hidden card is a 4. The player guesses Lower and scores another point.

Round 3: The GM's card is a 6. The player's hidden card is a 3. The player guesses Higher, but is wrong.

The player finishes with 2 points and gains a useful contact. The GM introduces a city guard who is willing to help the character resolve a minor offense and occasionally provide information about activity in the city.

If the player had scored 3 points, the GM could make the contact significantly more valuable—for example, a noble might invite the character to an upcoming party or auction, creating an opportunity for the entire party.

8. Explore the Settlement 🗺

Time: Half day · Skill: Perception, Investigation, History, Survival

Learn the town, city, wilderness edge, or surrounding region.

Choose a district, landmark, or local mystery to investigate. Success reveals services, shortcuts, rumors, hidden locations, local dangers, or useful NPCs.

Repeated exploration can build a simple settlement map and give the party an advantage when navigating familiar locations.

Rest & Recovery

Downtime is not automatically a Long Rest. A character can spend downtime working, fishing, researching, or socializing without receiving the full benefits of a Long Rest unless the GM says the conditions qualify.

When the party does take a Short or Long Rest, follow the recovery and card-shuffling rules in the Core Rules.

Downtime Design Principle

Downtime should create resources, relationships, information, and adventure hooks. Do not turn every activity into a lengthy skill challenge. Resolve simple activities with one check; use the fishing mini-game and other interactive systems when the players want to engage with them.
