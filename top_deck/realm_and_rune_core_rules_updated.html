1. Game Overview

Realm & Rune is a lightweight fantasy tabletop roleplaying game in which each player uses a standard deck of playing cards instead of dice. Characters are heroic adventurers who explore dangerous ruins, confront monsters, negotiate with rivals, uncover mysteries, and wield martial or magical abilities.

Core design principles: tactical hand management, low arithmetic, meaningful resource depletion, asymmetrical classes, and fast resolution.

2. Card System

2.1 Player Decks

Each player has their own standard 52-card deck. Jokers are an optional rule and are not part of the core game. Lower-level or introductory characters may begin with a reduced deck containing only number cards; the exact starting deck composition remains a playtest variable.

CardValueSpecial Rule
2–10Printed valueNormal card value.
Jack, Queen, King10May also trigger a face-card effect when an ability or spell says so.
Ace—Snake Eyes: an automatic failure when used for a check or attack. It has no additional consequence unless another rule creates one.
Joker (optional)—Reserved for an optional critical-fumble rule; not used in the core rules.

2.2 Card Locations

Deck

The active draw pile. When a rule tells you to draw, draw from the Deck.

Hand

Your current tactical resources. Cards in your Hand may be played for actions, reactions, attacks, skill checks, and abilities.

Shuffle Pile

Cards used for ordinary actions, attacks, reactions, and all skill checks go here. When the Deck is depleted, shuffle this pile to create a new Deck.

Burn Pile

Cards used to cast spells always go here. Burned cards are unavailable until recovered by rest or another rule.

Deck depletion: When the Deck is empty, shuffle the Shuffle Pile and make it the new Deck. The Burn Pile remains separate.
Short Rest: Shuffle all cards from the Shuffle Pile and Burn Pile back into the Deck. Long Rest: recover HP and shuffle all cards back into the Deck.

3. Character Creation

Create a character in this order:

  1. Choose an Ancestry. Ancestry does not change core attributes. Choose two ancestry feats you may develop or acquire.
  2. Choose a Background. A background grants two skill proficiencies from its short list and one unique feature or feat.
  3. Choose a Core Class.
  4. Assign core attributes from −1 to +3 according to the character-creation method used by the campaign.
  5. Choose skill proficiencies, equipment, and starting abilities.

3.1 Common Ancestries

The initial ancestry set is intended to use four familiar fantasy peoples. The exact feat lists remain open for development.

Human

Adaptable, ambitious, and broadly capable.

Elf

Long-lived, perceptive, and connected to ancient traditions.

Dwarf

Resilient, practical, and shaped by strong cultural traditions.

Halfling

Resourceful, mobile, and unusually capable of surviving danger.

5.2 Training Levels

TrainingProcedure
UntrainedDraw two cards and use the lower value. Add the relevant attribute modifier.
ProficientDraw one card, then play one card from your Hand. Use the played card’s value and add the relevant attribute modifier.
ExpertiseDraw one card, then play one or two cards from your Hand. If two cards are played, the second card must match the skill’s suit affinity. Add both card values and the relevant attribute modifier once.
Skill-check destination: All cards used during a skill check go to the Shuffle Pile, including face cards and all cards played for Expertise.
Snake Eyes: If an Ace is used as part of a skill check, the entire check automatically fails. No extra consequence occurs unless another rule says so.

Example: An expert in Athletics (Spade affinity) has Might +2. They play 8♥ first and 4♠ second: 8 + 4 + 2 = 14. The second card is valid because it is a Spade.

6. Scene & Turn Flow

6.1 Start of a Scene

At the start of a scene—combat, social encounter, exploration challenge, or other structured situation—each player draws up to their maximum Hand Size. Those cards become their current Hand.

6.2 Start of a Turn

At the start of each turn, draw one card. A character may then play as many cards as they choose, subject to their available actions and reactions. At the end of the turn, discard down to maximum Hand Size if necessary.

Design intent: Each turn guarantees access to at least one newly drawn card, while extra draws and increased Hand Size create opportunities for tactical bursts. Players may spend aggressively or preserve strong cards for later.

6.3 Action Economy

  • 1 Move: No card required.
  • Actions: One action may be taken for each card played, unless a rule says otherwise.
  • Reactions: A card must be played to trigger a reaction.
  • Card requirement: Every action or reaction requires a card unless a rule explicitly makes it free.

7. Combat

7.1 Attack Resolution

Play a card and add the relevant attribute modifier. If the result equals or exceeds the target’s AC, the attack hits.

Attack Total: Card Value + Relevant Attribute Modifier
Hit: Attack Total ≥ target AC.

An Ace automatically misses. Face cards have a value of 10 and may trigger a listed face-card effect.

7.2 Basic Actions

Might Strike

Basic Action Play one card and make a Might attack with a weapon in hand. On a hit, deal 1 HP. If the card’s suit matches the weapon’s suit category, deal 2 HP or gain the weapon’s listed perk.

Ranged Strike

Basic Action Play one card and make a Dexterity attack with a ranged weapon. On a hit, deal 1 HP. If the card’s suit matches the weapon’s suit category, deal 2 HP or gain the weapon’s listed perk.

Defend

Action Discard a card and add its value to your AC until the start of your next turn. The card goes to the Shuffle Pile.

Dodge

Reaction When hit, discard one Reserve card and negate 1 HP of damage. This may also be used to clear low-value Reserve cards. The card goes to the Shuffle Pile.

7.3 Example Suit Abilities

AbilityRule
Shield BashPlay a Spade. Make an attack; on a hit deal 1 HP and push the target or knock it prone. If a face card was played, the target is both pushed and prone.
Rallying CryPlay a Heart. One ally may draw a Reserve card. If a face card was played, two allies may draw a Reserve card.
BackstabPlay a Club. Make an attack; on a hit deal 2 HP. If a face card was played, deal 1 additional HP.
Arcane InsightPlay a Diamond. Reveal a weakness or useful insight about one enemy. If a face card was played, choose two enemies.

Reserve terminology: The current rules use a Hand as the primary tactical resource. “Reserve card” appears in some early ability text and should be standardized during the next rules edit—either as a separate reserve zone or as a defined subset of the Hand.

8.1 Examples

  • Unarmored character with Dexterity +0: AC 5.
  • Unarmored rogue with Dexterity +3: AC 8.
  • Rogue in light armor with Dexterity +3: AC 9.
  • Fighter in medium armor with a shield: AC 8.
  • Fighter in heavy armor with a shield: AC 10.

For early playtests, keep most common enemies at AC 5–7, trained enemies at AC 7–8, and elite enemies at AC 9–10. Make bosses durable and tactically interesting rather than giving them extremely high AC.

9.2 Casting a Spell

1. Choose the spell.
2. Burn a card matching its domain. Every card used to cast a spell goes to the Burn Pile.
3. Resolve the spell. Attack spells use the casting card’s value plus the spellcasting attribute. Other spells use their listed effect, save, or difficulty.
4. Apply Channel, if available.

For the initial Arcane design, magic attacks use Cunning:

Magic Attack: Burned Card Value + Cunning. If the total equals or exceeds AC, the spell hits.

Unless a spell says otherwise, the same matching-suit card both pays the casting cost and supplies the attack value. An Ace automatically fails when used for a spell attack.

9.3 Channel

Channel is a general spell keyword, not an automatic feature of every spell. A spell with Channel may be strengthened by burning additional cards matching its domain. Each spell states what extra cards do and how many may be used.

Recommended balance model: Channel is available only on spells that list it. Each spell sets its own maximum. This prevents unlimited burst damage while preserving the choice between several small spells and one powerful spell.

A class feature may improve Channel. For example, a Mage feature could improve Arcane Channel efficiency, increase a spell’s Channel limit, or reward the first additional Diamond burned.

11. Classes & Advancement

Characters advance from Level 1 to Level 6. The game then shifts to continued advancement through Feat Points rather than additional levels.

LevelUniversal Advancement
1Gain class identity features. Starting Hand Size: 2.
2Gain a subclass feature.
3Gain a class identity feature. Hand Size increases to 3.
4Gain a subclass feature.
5Increase any one core attribute by +1. Hand Size increases to 4.
6Gain a subclass feature.

Subclass design is intentionally deferred. Each subclass is planned to have a pool of 10 independent features rather than a rigid level-by-level list, allowing two characters with the same class and subclass to develop differently.

11.1 Core Classes

Four asymmetric core classes are planned. Their detailed level charts and subclass features remain separate development work and should be inserted here after finalization.

Fighter

Martial control, weapon mastery, defense, and sustained combat presence.

Rogue

Precision, mobility, tricks, opportunistic damage, and tactical card use.

Mage

Arcane versatility, spellcasting, and superior use of Channel.

Cleric

Divine support, healing, protection, leadership, and judgment.

11.2 Post-Level Advancement

After Level 6, characters earn Feat Points. Feat Points purchase new abilities, improve existing options, gain additional proficiencies or expertise, unlock ancestry features, or expand magical access. Costs and feat categories remain to be designed.

12. Open Playtest Questions & Next Development

High Priority

  • Define starting deck composition.
  • Standardize Hand versus Reserve terminology.
  • Define HP totals and damage expectations.
  • Write enemy statistics and encounter rules.
  • Finalize class identity features.
  • Define complete spell resolution rules.

Combat Tests

  • Does AC 5–10 produce the intended hit rate?
  • Is Defend too strong when high cards are available?
  • How often should weapon suit matching occur?
  • Is 1–3 HP Firebolt damage balanced?
  • How quickly do Burned spell cards create pressure?

Skill Tests

  • Are untrained checks too unreliable?
  • Is Expertise’s two-card total too large?
  • Are difficulty bands appropriate?
  • Does the suit requirement create useful choices?
  • Should the drawn card in trained checks have another purpose?
