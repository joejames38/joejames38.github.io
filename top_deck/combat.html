Realm & Rune
Combat Rules
I

Combat

Combat in Realm & Rune is fast, tactical, and driven by the cards in each player's personal deck.

Characters have a guaranteed opportunity to act each turn, but players can spend additional cards to create powerful turns when they have built up a strong hand.

Combat Principle

Every card spent is both an opportunity and a resource. Powerful turns can leave a character with fewer options on future turns.

II

Combat Round

Combat is divided into rounds. Within each round, combatants take turns according to the initiative order.

"Flip for Initiative"

Combat starts when the GM calls for "Flip for Initiative". Everyone plays a card from the top of their deck and adds their Initiative Bonus. The highest Initiative goes first with Players winning ties. Ties between two players is decided between the two players.

If a face card is drawn, you get an Acceleration bonus! Draw an extra card on your first turn!

Place played initiative cards in the discard pile. Place played face cards in the Burn pile.

  • Determine the order of combatants.
  • Each combatant takes a turn.
  • Resolve reactions when their triggering conditions occur.
  • Begin the next round when all combatants have acted.
    • III

    Your Turn

    At the beginning of your turn, draw one card from your Deck.

    You then have access to the following resources:

    Resource Rule
    Move Move your character. No card is required.
    Actions Play cards to perform actions. Normally, each card played allows one action.
    Reactions Play a card when the appropriate trigger occurs.

    You may continue playing cards and performing actions as long as you have cards available and valid actions to perform.

    At the end of your turn, discard cards until your Hand is at or below your maximum Hand Size.

    IV

    Movement

    Each character receives one Move each turn.

    Movement does not require a card and does not consume an Action.

    Movement Distance

    Exact movement distances may use grids, zones, or theater-of-the-mind in combat.

    Close: Right next to you. Touching distance, toe-to-toe in melee combat. Moving to something Close takes zero movement.

    Near: General room distance. Or about 30ft. A standard single movement allows you to travel from Close to Near.

    Far: Across a large room, down the hall, or a long distance away. Reaching a Far target usually takes a full movement plus a bit more or a special effort, putting targets at the edge of ranged weapon capabilities.

    Out of Range: Too far away to interact with or attack until you spend a turn moving close.

    V

    Attacks

    To make an attack, play a card and add the appropriate attribute modifier.

    Attack Total
    Card Value + Attribute Modifier

    If the Attack Total equals or exceeds the target's Armor Class, the attack hits.

    Unless an ability states otherwise, the card used for the attack is placed in the Shuffle Pile.

    VI

    Might Strike

    Basic Action: Might Strike

    Play: One card.

    Make a Might attack with a weapon you are wielding.

    Hit: Deal 1 HP of damage.

    Resonance Suit: If the card played matches the suit and is a Face Card associated with your weapon, deal 2 HP of damage instead and the card is placed in the Burn Pile.

    VII

    Ranged Strike

    Basic Action: Ranged Strike

    Play: One card.

    Make a Dexterity attack with a ranged weapon you are wielding.

    Hit: Deal 1 HP of damage.

    Resonance Suit: If the card played matches the suit and is a Face Card associated with your weapon, deal 2 HP of damage instead and the card is placed in the Burn Pile.

    VIII

    Weapons & Suit Affinity

    Weapons are divided into four broad categories associated with the four suits.

    Suit Weapon Theme Example
    ♠ Spades Melee / martial weapons Swords
    ♥ Hearts Supportive / ceremonial weapons Maces
    ♣ Clubs Agile / precision weapons Daggers, bows
    ♦ Diamonds Technical / magical weapons Wands

    Any character may use any weapon unless a rule states otherwise. Characters proficient with a weapon gain its associated benefits.

    Weapon Mastery

    When you have weapon mastery with a certain suit weapons, you gain resonance with all cards of that suit instead of just face cards, and you discard the card instead of burning the card.

    Weapon Mastery can be gained in each of the 4 suits.

    IX

    Armor & Defense

    Armor Class determines how difficult a character is to hit.

    Armor Armor Class
    Unarmored 5 + Dexterity
    Light Armor 6 + Dexterity
    Medium Armor 7
    Heavy Armor 9
    Shield +1 AC
    Example
    Dexterity +3 Rogue, Unarmored = AC 8
    Medium Armor + Shield = AC 8
    Heavy Armor + Shield = AC 10
    X

    Defend

    Basic Action: Defend

    Play: One card.

    Discard the card and add its value to your AC until the beginning of your next turn.

    The card is placed in the Shuffle Pile.

    Defend represents taking a defensive stance, using your weapon or shield to protect yourself, or otherwise preparing for incoming attacks.

    XI

    Dodge

    Reaction: Dodge

    Trigger: You are hit by an attack.

    Cost: Discard one Reserve card.

    Effect: Negate 1 HP of damage from the attack.

    Reserve

    Reserve cards represent cards intentionally held outside the normal Hand for reactions and special abilities. The exact Reserve rules are being developed separately.

    XII

    Rallying Cry

    General Action: Rallying Cry

    Play: One Heart.

    Choose one ally. That ally may draw one card from their Reserve.

    Face Card: If the Heart played is a face card, choose two allies. Each ally may draw one card from their Reserve.

    Purpose

    Rallying Cry is intended to make Spirit characters and supportive characters valuable without requiring a dedicated healing class. It allows one character to help another manage their card resources.

    XIII

    Gobble

    Aces are not automatic failures. Instead, they provide access to a special consumable mechanic.

    Special Action: Gobble

    Cost: Burn one Ace.

    Effect: Consume one food, drink, potion, or other consumable item in your inventory.

    The Ace is placed directly into the Burn Pile.

    Consumable Effects

    Consumables may provide an immediate effect, a temporary effect, or a persistent effect.

    Persistent Food Effects

    A character may have only one persistent food effect active at a time.

    Consuming a new item that provides a persistent food effect immediately ends the previous persistent food effect and replaces it with the new one.

    Example

    A fighter consumes a Protein Bar and gains +1 Might until their next Long Rest. Later, they consume a Fizzy Drink. The +1 Might effect ends and is replaced by +1 Dexterity.

    Example Consumables

    Item Effect
    Bread Recover 1 HP.
    Health Potion Recover 3 HP.
    Protein Bar +1 Might until your next Long Rest.
    Fizzy Drink +1 Dexterity until your next Long Rest.
    Arcane Tea +1 Cunning until your next Long Rest.
    Comfort Stew +1 Spirit until your next Long Rest.
    Trail Mix Recover 1 HP and gain +1 Survival until your next Long Rest.
    Herbal Tonic Recover 2 HP.
    Ironbrew +1 AC until your next Long Rest.
    Swiftroot Juice Increase your movement by 1 until your next Long Rest.
    Design Note

    The consumable list will expand as the equipment and exploration systems are developed.

    XIV

    Damage & HP

    Attacks and abilities normally deal a small number of HP. This keeps combat fast and makes individual attacks meaningful.

    Current Damage Scale

    Basic successful attacks generally deal 1 HP. Weapon affinity, class features, critical effects, and special abilities can increase this amount.

    Zero HP

    The exact rules for characters reaching 0 HP, including unconsciousness, death, stabilization, and recovery, remain a playtest item.

    XV

    Face Cards in Combat

    Jacks, Queens, and Kings have a value of 10.

    Some combat actions provide an additional effect when a face card is played.

    Card Value Special Rule
    Jack 10 May activate face-card abilities.
    Queen 10 May activate face-card abilities.
    King 10 May activate face-card abilities.
    Ace 1* May be Burned to perform Gobble.

    *The exact numerical value of an Ace when used in a normal resolution remains a playtest consideration. Its defining special function is access to Gobble.

    XVI

    Combat Card Management

    Cards spent during combat are normally moved to the Shuffle Pile.

    Use Destination
    Attack Shuffle Pile
    Basic Action Shuffle Pile
    Reaction Shuffle Pile unless otherwise stated
    Skill Check Shuffle Pile
    Spell Burn Pile
    Gobble Burn Pile
    Resource Tension

    Combat is intentionally designed around a tension between spending cards now and preserving cards for future turns. Effects that draw additional cards should therefore be valuable and relatively limited.

    XVII

    Combat Playtest Notes

    The following elements should be specifically tested during early playtests:

    • Average number of successful attacks per round.
    • Whether AC produces approximately 50% hit rates against appropriate-level enemies.
    • How frequently players spend their entire Hand.
    • Whether additional card-draw abilities create excessively explosive turns.
    • Whether Gobble makes Aces feel valuable without making consumables too powerful.
    • Whether persistent food buffs are strong enough to be interesting without becoming mandatory.
    • Whether Rallying Cry creates meaningful team play.
    • Whether Defend competes effectively with offensive actions.
    • Whether Dodge provides sufficient defensive value for the Reserve resource.
    • Appropriate HP values for player characters and enemies.
