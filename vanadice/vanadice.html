Welcome to Vanadice

Vanadice is a tabletop fantasy roleplaying game built upon the revised 2024 rules of Dungeons & Dragons. Players take on the roles of adventurers who explore dangerous lands, undertake quests, battle monsters, and uncover forgotten secrets.

The defining feature of Vanadice is its job system. Rather than relying exclusively on traditional D&D classes, players choose from jobs inspired by Final Fantasy XI. Each job represents a distinct combat role, magical discipline, or specialized approach to adventuring.

Every core job advances from level 1 to level 10. Players can further customize their characters by selecting a subjob, providing additional abilities and expanding their tactical options.

Core Principles

Core Jobs

Choose a primary job that determines your character's progression, combat identity, and primary abilities.

Subjobs

Supplement your core job with one of three available subjobs, creating distinctive combinations.

Level 10 Cap

Each job progresses through ten levels. Character development emphasizes meaningful abilities rather than extended level progression.

Spell Points

Spellcasting uses a spell point system instead of traditional spell slots, allowing players greater flexibility in how they expend magical resources.

Core Jobs & Subjobs

Vanadice draws inspiration from Final Fantasy XI's combination of main jobs and support jobs. A character's core job defines their primary identity, while their subjob offers supplementary abilities.

Core Job

Your core job is the primary source of your character's features, hit points, proficiencies, and progression. Each core job advances independently from level 1 to level 10.

Subjob

Each core job will have three associated subjob options. A subjob provides a secondary set of abilities that complement or contrast with the core job.

Subjob features, progression limits, and their interaction with core job levels will be defined in the individual class rules.

Subjobs are a Vanadice-specific system and do not automatically follow the standard D&D multiclass rules. See the individual job descriptions for finalized requirements.

Ability Scores

Vanadice uses the six standard D&D ability scores. These represent the fundamental physical and mental capabilities of your character.

Strength

Physical power, lifting, carrying, and powerful melee attacks.

Dexterity

Agility, reflexes, coordination, and precision.

Constitution

Endurance, physical resilience, and resistance to hardship.

Intelligence

Reasoning, memory, knowledge, and analytical ability.

Wisdom

Perception, intuition, awareness, and understanding.

Charisma

Confidence, force of personality, leadership, and influence.

Ability modifiers, saving throws, skill checks, and ability score improvements use the 2024 D&D framework unless specifically modified by a Vanadice rule.

Skills & Proficiency

Skills represent specialized training and experience. Vanadice uses the standard 2024 D&D skill system.

When making a skill check, roll a twenty-sided die (d20) and add the relevant ability modifier. If proficient in the skill, add your proficiency bonus.

Skill Check = d20 + Ability Modifier + Proficiency Bonus

The Dungeon Master determines the Difficulty Class (DC) based on the challenge.

  • DC 5 – Very Easy
  • DC 10 – Easy
  • DC 15 – Moderate
  • DC 20 – Hard
  • DC 25 – Very Hard
  • DC 30 – Nearly Impossible

Combat

Combat uses the initiative, action economy, movement, attack rolls, saving throws, and damage rules of D&D 2024 unless a specific Vanadice feature states otherwise.

Initiative

At the beginning of combat, each participant rolls initiative using a Dexterity check. Participants act in descending initiative order.

Turn Structure

On your turn, you can typically:

  • Move up to your Speed.
  • Take one action.
  • Use a bonus action when an ability permits it.
  • Interact with an object as permitted by the rules.
  • Take reactions when their triggers occur.

Attacks

An attack generally involves rolling a d20, adding the appropriate ability modifier and proficiency bonus when applicable, and comparing the result against the target's Armor Class.

Damage & Hit Points

Hit points represent a character's ability to withstand physical harm, exhaustion, and other dangers. Damage reduces current hit points. Healing restores hit points up to the character's maximum.

Conditions

Conditions such as Blinded, Charmed, Frightened, Grappled, Incapacitated, Invisible, Paralyzed, Poisoned, Prone, Restrained, Stunned, and Unconscious use the 2024 D&D rules.

Spellcasting & Spell Points

Vanadice replaces traditional spell slots with the Spell Points variant system. This allows spellcasters to spend a shared pool of magical energy to cast spells of different levels.

Spell Points

Spellcasting Rules

  • Each spell has a spell level and corresponding spell point cost.
  • A character must have access to the appropriate spell level to cast that spell.
  • Spell points are expended when a spell is cast.
  • Spell points are recovered according to the character's designated recovery rules.
  • Cantrips do not expend spell points.
  • Spell components, concentration, casting times, and spell effects follow D&D 2024 unless modified.

Spell Point Recovery

The amount of spell points recovered through short rests, long rests, or other abilities will be established by the individual job rules and the Vanadice spellcasting framework.

The spell point costs above are a proposed Vanadice casting-cost schedule. The total spell point pool, maximum spell level, and recovery rules still need to be established for the level 10 job system. This is distinct from the official D&D DMG Spell Points variant, which uses its own point costs and progression table.

Resting & Recovery

Short Rest

A short rest is a period of at least one hour during which a character performs no strenuous activity. Characters can spend Hit Point Dice to recover hit points according to the applicable rules.

Long Rest

A long rest is an extended period of downtime that restores a character's resources and capabilities according to the 2024 D&D rules and any Vanadice-specific modifications.

Death & Dying

When a character reaches 0 hit points, they fall unconscious and begin making death saving throws unless an effect states otherwise.

Death saving throws, stabilization, healing, and resurrection use the 2024 D&D framework. Individual job abilities may provide exceptions.

The Game Master

The Game Master (GM) creates adventures, controls non-player characters, manages encounters, and adjudicates rules.

The GM determines the difficulty of challenges, interprets uncertain situations, and ensures that the game remains engaging and fair.
